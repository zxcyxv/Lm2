# Epoch-3 flow and generation audit

## Result

At step 10071, the model has not learned the causal gold-encoder velocity,
either in the raw carrier or on the normalized carrier ray.  It has instead
learned a much smaller correction to the unitary conditioning orbit.  That
uncorrected orbit begins close to the gold latent direction but drifts away
with horizon.

This makes a latent velocity objective plausible as an additional training
constraint; it does not show that the present CE-only model is already a flow
matching model.

## Checkpoint state

The epoch-3 training report is:

- training CE: 2.89149
- validation block NLL: 2.88853
- validation block byte accuracy: 0.23932
- H1 NLL / accuracy: 1.51911 / 0.56348
- H16 NLL / accuracy: 3.11989 / 0.19238
- gradient norm: 0.60790

The optimization itself is stable at this checkpoint.  The long-horizon gap
is not explained by a contemporaneous gradient explosion.

## Raw-carrier velocity

For a causal encoder trajectory `g_r` and hidden rotation `R`, the registered
target and predicted increments were

```text
delta*_r = g_r - R g_(r-1)
delta_r  = z_r - R z_(r-1).
```

Both were moved into the same co-rotating frame before comparison.  Since this
frame change is orthogonal, it changes coordinates but not their cosine or
norm ratio.

Across 64 fixed validation windows and 16 horizons (1024 observations):

- mean velocity cosine: -0.01205
- sample-clustered standard error of the mean: 0.00632
- mean relative error: 1.00063
- mean predicted increment norm: 2.81955
- mean target increment norm: 193.94352

There is no positive raw-velocity alignment.  Correct bytes do not rescue the
hypothesis: their mean cosine is -0.03288, versus -0.00554 for incorrect
bytes.  Horizon-conditioned differences change sign and do not form a
consistent correctness effect.

## Ray-geometry control

The decoder consumes a fixed-RMS representative, so raw radial mismatch alone
would be insufficient.  The follow-up therefore compared

```text
v_ray_r = normalize(z_r) - R normalize(z_(r-1))
v*_ray_r = normalize(g_r) - R normalize(g_(r-1)).
```

The radial-gauge control reaches the same conclusion:

- mean ray-velocity cosine: 0.00189
- mean ray-velocity relative error: 0.99969
- mean predicted ray-step norm: 0.001584
- mean target ray-step norm: 0.130359

Thus the predicted directional step is about 82 times smaller in norm and is
not aligned with the gold directional step.  This is not merely a carrier
scale artifact.

The endpoint direction contains a weaker positive result.  Mean
`cos(normalize(z_r), normalize(g_r))` is 0.81957.  It falls monotonically in
the important region:

| Horizon | State-direction cosine | Predicted ray-step norm | Gold ray-step norm |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.99036 | 0.006302 | 0.135686 |
| 4 | 0.95798 | 0.002608 | 0.128734 |
| 8 | 0.86179 | 0.000274 | 0.121567 |
| 12 | 0.72069 | 0.000552 | 0.131739 |
| 16 | 0.56603 | 0.000666 | 0.128199 |

The unitary base orbit is already close to the next causal encoder state at
H1.  The learned innovation barely moves that orbit, however, while the gold
trajectory continues to move by roughly 0.13 per horizon on the unit sphere.
The accumulated missing correction explains the steady endpoint drift.

Overall state-direction cosine is 0.86045 on correct bytes and 0.80678 on
incorrect bytes, but this is substantially confounded by shallow horizons
being easier.  Restricted to H1-H4 it is 0.97974 for correct and 0.97423 for
incorrect bytes.  The small conditional gap does not support state cosine as
a strong correctness explanation yet.

## Free generation

The causally correct final-prefix anchor (position 255) was used.  This avoids
the prior mistake of treating the last stride-16 training anchor at position
240 as the continuation root.

Over eight fixed prompts and 128 generated bytes:

| Method | Mean exact-byte accuracy | Mean unique byte values | Qualitative result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| H1, re-anchor after every byte | 0.07324 | 18.75 | word-like English fragments, but repetitive generic templates and little prompt continuation |
| H16, commit each 16-byte block | 0.19238 | 5.25 | collapse primarily to spaces plus a few common bytes; not sentences |

The higher H16 exact-byte score is not evidence of better language.  Its very
low byte diversity and the recorded samples show that it exploits common
whitespace bytes.  H1 produces superficially more language-like strings, for
example repeated constructions around `the southern`, `the second`, and
`the compound`, but none of the eight samples sustains the prompt's facts or
syntax for 128 bytes.

At three epochs the model has learned local byte and WikiText-style surface
statistics, not robust autoregressive continuation.  Teacher-forced H1
accuracy therefore does not transfer to long free-running quality.

## Consequence for flow matching

The cleanest reading is:

1. CE only needs a decoder-effective latent representative; it does not force
   the central path to reproduce the encoder's causal latent trajectory.
2. The current unitary schedule supplies a good H1 neighborhood almost by
   itself.
3. The learned measurement innovation becomes too small to correct later
   drift, matching the observed H1-to-H16 degradation.

A target-side auxiliary can enforce the missing conjugacy without changing
the inference recurrence or its parallel scan.  The first controlled test
should use a small relative ray-velocity or endpoint-direction loss together
with CE, with gold encoder states detached (or supplied by a frozen/EMA target
encoder).  Direct unscaled MSE on the raw increment is not justified here:
the observed target norm is about two orders of magnitude larger and the
decoder is radially normalized.

This proposed auxiliary is discrete latent velocity matching.  Calling it
full distributional flow matching would require an explicit stochastic or
interpolating path and a velocity field evaluated along that path.  Adding
state-dependent continuous-flow feedback would be a different mechanism and
could destroy the present time-varying scan; the diagnostic does not justify
that change.
