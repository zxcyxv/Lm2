# Epoch-2 matched H4 generation: 13M versus 121M

## Status

- State: completed
- Training and fixed validation prompts only; test unread

The 121M H1--H16 mean rollout CE was `3.638637377`, versus
`3.494413535` for 13M. The respective accuracies were `0.189758301` and
`0.203552246`. The 121M free generations were more byte-diverse and repeated
fewer 4-grams, but the twelve retained samples did not show better sentence
quality and included worse invalid-byte and long-run failures.

## Question and comparison

At the identical two-epoch-equivalent boundary, does increasing the shared
encoder/feedback width from 1344 (13M) to 4352 (121M) improve matched
sixteen-byte rollout CE and free generation quality for the H4-trained
state-dependent feedback architecture?

The comparison uses the preserved 13M H4 step-6,714 evidence and the new 121M
H4 step-6,714 checkpoint. Both models use their natural greedy block-4 policy.
No H1 or parallel-scan model is mixed into the primary ranking.

## Fixed protocol

- step 6,714 checkpoints; seed 1337 training lineage
- the same 64 fixed validation prompts and 256-byte prompt context
- matched rollout uses anchors `0,16,...,240`; every sixteen-target interval
  starts from the literal gold prefix, then four greedy outputs are appended
  and canonically re-encoded until the next gold boundary
- score all original H1--H16 target bytes: 1,024 labels/horizon and 16,384
  labels/model; this is gold-aligned rollout CE, historically called block NLL,
  rather than a teacher-forced likelihood
- 128 generated bytes per prompt; greedy block-4 generation
- every generated four-byte chunk is appended, then the last 256 bytes are
  canonically re-encoded; no gold token is inserted after the prompt
- report the same twelve escaped prompt/continuation samples for both widths
- scalar metrics: aligned first-128 accuracy, corpus byte entropy, unique-byte
  count, repeated-4gram fraction, longest identical-byte run, strict UTF-8
  fraction, top1--top2 margin, and predictive entropy
- checkpoint and validation-start SHA-256 values recorded in `run.tsv`
- horizon, block, generation, and comparison metrics in TSV; samples in TSV;
  interpretation in Markdown
- checkpoints remain ignored; test split remains unread

## Interpretation criteria

- lower repetition and identical-byte runs, together with maintained or higher
  entropy/diversity, count as evidence against collapse
- readable samples are retained alongside collapsed or malformed samples
- teacher-forced NLL and free-generation quality are reported separately; one
  does not substitute for the other
- the 121M checkpoint must reproduce its registered full-context H1--H4 rows
  within `2e-3`; all 16,384 prefix-reencoded rollout scores must be finite
- differences are attributed only to this matched width comparison and do not
  establish a general parameter-scaling law

## Prefix-shape audit amendment

The initial pre-run gate incorrectly required the requested variable-length
prefix re-encoding path to match the training producer's single full-context
encoding within `2e-3`. The width-4352 checkpoint differed by `0.05257049`
with rollout microbatch 8 and by `0.05327323` with microbatch 4. Changing the
batch size therefore did not remove the discrepancy.

The two paths are causally equivalent in exact arithmetic but execute different
float32 GEMM shapes: the registered evaluator encodes all 256 context bytes
once and selects every anchor, whereas block re-entry canonically encodes the
literal prefix of each anchor and generated chunk. The checkpoint-identity gate
now reruns the original full-context evaluator at its registered microbatch 4.
The requested rollout retains the completed 13M producer's microbatch 8 and
records its prefix-reencode-versus-full-context delta instead of suppressing it.
